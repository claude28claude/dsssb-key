What this is. The static General Awareness questions DSSSB has actually asked in its computer-based tests since 2021, sorted by subject. The papers come from DSSSB's own “Question Bank Online Exam” pages and from official papers re-hosted by coaching sites: ASO 2025 and Patwari 2022, the clerical and office posts (Junior Assistant, Junior Clerk, Head Clerk, Junior Secretariat Assistant, Stenographer, Assistant Superintendent, MTS and others), the PRT, TGT and PGT teaching papers, and the General Awareness sections of technical and senior posts. Within each topic, ASO and Patwari questions come first, then clerical posts.

Where the answers come from. Each official DSSSB question paper marks the correct option with a green tick. That tick was read straight from the paper by a program, which checks every question against its printed question ID — the answers were never typed in by hand. Where the key disagrees with standard textbooks, the question shows a note saying so; where DSSSB's key is plainly wrong, the question was left out.

What was left out. Current affairs (events, appointments, awards and reports from the year of the exam), reasoning, maths and language questions, questions whose facts have since changed, and papers that could not be read reliably — scanned pages, papers printed only in Punjabi, Urdu or Sanskrit, and papers whose questions are pictures. Questions asked in Hindi are shown in English translation.

MTS 2026. A separate section holds the General Awareness questions from all 117 shifts of the MTS exam (24 February – 14 May 2026), read from a compilation of the published answer keys. On the shifts that also exist as DSSSB's own ticked papers, every matching answer agreed. Current affairs and unclear questions were left out.

AS 2026. Another separate section holds the General Awareness questions from all 17 shifts of the Assistant Superintendent exam (1 – 7 July 2026), read from a coaching institute's compilation of the answer key. DSSSB's own ticked papers for these shifts are not available, so every answer was checked again, and questions whose key looked wrong or unclear were left out along with current affairs.
